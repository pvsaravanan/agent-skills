# Opener concepts: choose a skeleton, do not inherit a skeleton

Read for openers, product promos, intros, and teasers BEFORE writing the rundown.

Without a choice of skeleton, openers for different products tend to come out with the same
arrangement (text growing from a zoom then exiting left, three feature icon tiles, a typed
input box, then hook → feature → feature → promise → logo → CTA), even when the palette and
font have changed. The cause: a pattern that worked once is easy to imitate, and a prohibition
that has only one fix makes every way out land on the same answer.
Law #3 locks the novelty of the SKIN; this document locks the novelty of the SKELETON.

## The skeleton laws

1. **Three candidates, one choice.** Before the rundown, write three candidate concepts from
   the menu below (or a new concept), each a one-sentence premise that names
   the product. Pick one; the reason comes from the nature of the product: *what is most convincing
   when SEEN*, not what is easiest to build. Show all three to the user
   together with the style brief.
2. **The structure fingerprint.** Write one line:
   `concept · scene count · main object of each scene · opening · closing`.
   Put it in the style brief and in the closing message, so the next project can
   compare.
3. **Different from the previous opener.** The previous opener = the one in the conversation,
   in memory, or named by the user. The concept must differ, OR at least three of the five
   fingerprint elements differ. A new color over an old scene order is still a template.
4. **Canonical components are not defaults.** This list is not forbidden, but each item
   may appear only when the concept demands it, and at most TWO in one opener:
   - text enlarged from a zoom then exiting left;
   - three feature tiles/cards side by side;
   - an input box / searchbar being typed into;
   - a camera push-through between scenes;
   - a light leak at the cut point;
   - a logo + CTA stacked in the center as the last scene.
5. **A fixed order is not a standard structure.** Hook → feature → feature → promise → logo → CTA
   is only one possibility, and may not be used in two openers in a row.
6. **A scene does not have to be "a sentence + an object".** A scene may have no text (product
   action), may be text only (typography), may be several objects reacting to each other.
   What is guarded is still anti-PPT: not three text levels, not faded sections.
7. **Examples are not scripts.** The order of acts in concepts 13–19 is an example of how to chain
   their motion language, not a script. When the user asks for "style/concept X":
   - take its PRINCIPLES and MOTION LANGUAGE (visual materials, how it transitions, rhythm, color), then
     compose new acts from the user's product and audience;
   - the opening and closing are NOT the same as that concept's example; pick from the menu or
     derive them from the brand;
   - a moment marked ✦ (a distinctive moment) at most one, and it must be transformed:
     its words, shape, and meaning from the brand, not the same moment with the objects swapped;
   - the resulting fingerprint differs by ≥ 3 of 5 elements from that concept's example fingerprint;
   - the palette, flat shapes, and props are always derived from the logo, palette, and world of
     the user's brand; if the brand has only one color, the accent is chosen with a written reason;
   - if the user explicitly asks for a specific example moment, it may be used; say
     that the moment was taken from the example.

## Object relay: deriving it from the brand


Concept 13 is the easiest to fit to a brand because its contents are taken from the journey
of that brand's own service, not from a template:

1. **Map the service journey into acts**: a need appears → choosing → collecting →
   processing/packing → arriving. Drop acts that are not distinctive to the brand; merge the ones that are too thin.
2. **One hero object** that changes form but stays recognizable: an item on screen →
   a product card being held → a real object → the package contents → a package at its destination.
3. **An actor from the brand's world, may be unexpected.** It does not have to be "a person holding a phone":
   the actual beneficiary can be the actor (e.g. an animal shopping for
   itself in a pet service). The humor is born from the brand, not tacked on.
   A hand/foot/paw entering the frame is enough; a whole character is not required.
4. **Props from the brand's product category.** Small objects (snacks, tools, ingredients,
   packaging) are taken from what the brand really sells/serves; that is what makes the
   opener feel like the brand's own even without text.
5. **The brand color as the through-line.** The brand's main color is on the most important object of every
   act (a uniform, device, card, package), while the background may change light ↔
   dark ↔ white per act. An opening focus technique: the world drawn in gray lines, only the
   actor/brand in color.
6. **A diegetic brand, an end card is not required.** The brand name sticks to the world (writing on a
   wall/table, a screen, a package label); the closing can simply be the receiving action.
7. **Act rhythm.** The opening and closing are short (±2 s); the choosing/collecting acts are the
   longest (3–4.5 s). Each act has one main action that reads with no sound and no text.

## Brand system showcase: layout, color, rhythm


Concept 14 suits when what is being sold is the identity (a launch, a rebrand, a community).
Its contents are taken from the user's own brand assets (logo, symbol, mascot, community language,
brand guideline pages) so two brands cannot produce the same video.

**An example order** (not a script; skeleton law #7):
1. An empty frame → a motif object (the logo shape made thick and layered) rises from the bottom edge,
   its layers following one by one; personality stickers stick to its corners.
2. The object condenses into one icon; the icon's contents swap quickly several times then stop on
   the brand symbol; the wordmark is revealed from behind the symbol.
3. The logo rests in the center; a palette color field rises/sweeps in stages (the secondary briefly, then
   the main one in full) and the logo flips color in place following the field that covers it.
4. The symbol alone on the main color field, breathing with light/an accent from the brand's meaning.
5. The camera passes through the symbol → a neutral background; a manifesto appears word by word, 1–2 lines.
6. An asset parade (a row of tiles/icons/mascots) enters from the side pushing the text out; one
   asset stops in the center and is crowded with stickers.
7. The assets spin until thin; a second color field card spins open to fill the frame;
   a second manifesto.
8. Brand guideline pages (logo, color, typography, graphics, applications) rise stacked from
   the bottom covering the text; a last blank page covers the frame.
9. The symbol grows from a single point; stickers burst from behind it then are pulled back.
10. The symbol gets the same thick layers as in act 1, then exits through the bottom edge
    → an empty frame = the first frame (loop).

**Layout**
- One central axis in almost all acts; the main object ±25–35 % of the frame height, the rest
  empty space.
- Small-medium text (±6 % of the frame height per line), at most two lines, centered,
  lowercase/sentence case, tight tracking.
- Stickers (chat bubbles, labels, emoji) stick to an object's corner, tilted ±8–15°,
  riding the object's edge, sized ±25–40 % of the object.
- The brand guideline pages are used LARGE (70–90 % of the frame width) and tilted in perspective;
  read as a picture, not as text.

**Color**
- A light neutral background (white with a thin tint, a slight gradient) takes ±55 % of the screen area
  throughout the video; the brand color feels strong because it is rare.
- The brand color appears FULL only in two acts (±3 s each) as chapter markers:
  the main color in the logo act, the secondary color in the second manifesto act.
- Outside those two fields, the brand color only sticks to objects (icons, stickers, thick
  layers). 3D objects may have glossy gradients to contrast with the flat fields.
- Text almost black, on both the neutral background and color fields; the logo flips white if
  the field is saturated enough.

**Rhythm**
- No hard cuts; 25–30 s, looping. Acts of 2.5–4 s; two energy peaks: the main color field
  entering, and the camera passing through the symbol.
- Continuous music; each big move lands on a beat; the manifesto appears word by word following
  the beat.

## Claim → how → result: layout, color, rhythm


Concept 15 suits when what is sold is a new capability and its result can be shown.
The product itself appears as it is: the real UI, a cursor, real work output. Its luxury
comes from restraint: almost no color, no decoration, slow motion.

**An example order** (not a script; skeleton law #7)
1. The product's output opens very close and blurred, then moves away shrinking and fading
   into the background while the first claim sentence appears beneath it.
2. A claim of 1–2 lines; a short line "with [product name/logo]" follows.
3. UI components (an input bar, a panel) appear small with no device frame, shift to the
   center, then the camera zooms to the part being used.
4. A big cursor becomes the actor: dragging an asset into a slot, opening a menu, choosing an option.
5. The result slot detaches as an empty gray card, blooms to the center, then the card starts
   playing the result and grows to nearly fill the frame; the camera slowly passes through its contents.
6. The card shrinks while its contents keep being cropped in → a pill → a dot; the next claim
   sentence appears exactly at that dot.
7. Acts 3–6 repeat for the second feature (and the third if any), with different UI components.
8. A quality claim → a site address typed → the address erased → the product name typed,
   still for 2–3 s → fading into the background.

**Layout**
- One central axis. Text in one style: a neutral regular-weight grotesk, sentence case, dark
  gray; line height ±8 % of the frame height, at most two lines, block width 30–65 %.
- Text font = the product's own UI font, so the video feels like an extension of the product
  (an exception to the "display font with character" in Law #3).
- The UI floats with no device frame, rounded corners, a wide soft shadow; during the
  demo the UI is zoomed to ±80 % of the frame width and only the part being used is visible.
- The result card at its peak is 75–90 % of the frame width and height, rounded corners, a soft
  shadow; its contents always move.

**Color**
- ±80 % of the screen area an impure warm white, ±15 % neutral gray (UI, shadows),
  ±5 % color. Color comes ONLY from the product's work output; the logo and UI are monochrome.
- The background is still layered even though it is almost white: a warm white + a radial light brighter in
  the center + one curved hairline that occasionally crosses behind the text.
- Suits products whose output is colorful. If the product's output is not visual, use another
  concept.

**Rhythm**
- No cuts, calm motion (ease in-out 0.8–1.2 s), small per-second frame changes.
  A claim 1–2.5 s, a how 2–6 s, a result 4–6 s; the cycle 2–3 times; a closing of 4–5 s.
- Claim words appear in sequence from blurred-gray to sharp-dark; a sentence exits by
  dimming to gray first, then disappears.

## Through gradient shapes: layout, color, rhythm


Concept 18 suits brands that want to feel deep and mysterious with few sentences.
Its skeleton comes from one brand-owned shape (a logo silhouette, a letter, a symbol, or a shape from
the product's meaning: a keyhole for security, an arch for doors/access) repeated
in layers. Pick the part that fits the product.

**An example order** (not a script; skeleton law #7)
1. Dark; a single small point of light appears.
2. The point blooms into gradient shape layers rotating slowly (repeating petals,
   rings, layered arcs).
3. A small light/glass orb bounces in and becomes the eye guide.
4. The camera follows the orb through thin-line layers (arcs or repeating frames)
   like a tunnel.
5. Small shapes (a triangle, a cube, a sweep) orbit briefly: the product's contents in an
   abstract language.
6. A portal/shape hole opens; inside it the brand's symbol or object glowing.
7. The camera passes through the hole into dark → the logo, or an object falls onto a podium.

**Layout**
- One central axis; shape layers 80–120 % of the frame height (clipped by the edge), their center
  slightly above or below the middle.
- A small guiding orb (±3–6 % of the frame height) is the only sharp object moving
  fast.
- Very little text: small at the side of an object, or none until the logo.

**Color**
- A dark base or a deep color from the brand palette ±60–70 % of the area; a glowing gradient from one
  bright palette color to the base color, or two colors far apart alternating per layer.
- A fine grain across the whole frame (grainy gradient, `techniques.md` §7d).
- One light accent only on the guiding orb and the portal core.

**Rhythm**
- Calm: ease in-out 1–2 s, layers rotating 10–20° per second, the camera entering 0.8–1.2 s
  per layer. No cuts; the peak is passing through the portal.

## Flat pop with photos: layout, color, rhythm


Concept 17 suits consumer brands that sell a feeling: cheerful, close, everyday.
Its skeleton comes from three of the user's brand materials: photos of people (users/ambassadors), flat shapes
derived from the logo, and the brand palette used as full color fields.
Pick the parts that fit; do not copy it whole.

**The motion language of this style** (what is taken when the user asks for "flat pop with photos")
- cut-out people photos that pop elastically and are often clipped by the bottom edge;
- flat shapes derived from the logo as containers for photos, stickers, and rays;
- brand color fields that change quickly with a visible trigger;
- two-scale text: small labels that stick to objects and a big word behind the object;
- a montage on the beat with the subject's position fixed.
The acts below are only an example of how ONE video chains that language.

**An example order** (not a script; skeleton law #7; ✦ = a distinctive moment, at most one and it must be transformed)
1. ✦ An empty light frame; a short greeting typed, one of its letters stretched repeatedly
   until it fills the frame width, closed with an emoticon.
2. A person (a cut-out photo) pops from below; props in the brand color stick on (accessories,
   stickers); label pills of words around the head; small flat shapes (hearts, stars) burst upward.
3. A single label pill in the center blooms into a shape container (a blob/circle) holding the
   next person; a flat ray or lightning bursts behind it.
4. The next person in a rounded-square frame in the brand color; the old frame is pushed
   out; the camera pushes into a color field until that color becomes the full background.
5. ✦ One UI element (a toggle/button) on a color field; when activated, the background color
   changes with it: the background changes because of an action, not because of a cut.
6. The UI element shrinks into the screen of a phone in a hand.
7. ✦ A giant word (the user's role/profession) clipped by the edge behind the person; the word, the person,
   and the phone screen contents swap together 2–4 times.
8. A small problem: one word + an object (a laptop/bag covered in stickers) on a light
   background; the object flies to the lens with motion blur → a second color field.
9. A folder/box drops from above, then feature illustrations (a chart, a money bag, a photo
   card) burst out of it.
10. One photo card grows to fill the frame → a person on a white background.
11. An app icon appears above the person and grows into a color field; pieces of the logo
    shape spin; palette color fields alternate quickly (0.2–0.3 s per color).
12. ✦ The wordmark assembled on a color field: a thin construction grid, ghost outline letters
    scattered about, letters flying into place one by one; the camera zooms until the letters
    are clipped by the edge.
13. The brand in the real world: photos of places/usage locations changing quickly, brand
    stickers/markers staying in the same position.
14. Brand merchandise used by people, giant pale brand letters/symbol behind them.
15. The brand in public space (a night billboard) — the only dark background.
16. ✦ Giant brand letters/symbol as a stage: people posing through its shape (partly in front,
    partly behind), letter colors alternating per beat.
17. Closing: a phone entering from the side, the wordmark in the corner, a tagline word by word,
    a group of people cheering, one small sticker.

**Layout**
- Big people: 60–100 % of the frame height, often clipped by the bottom edge, asymmetric
  position (left/right of center); one frame = one person + 1–3 flat elements.
- Only two text scales: small label pills (±5 % of the frame height) that stick to objects, and
  a giant word (±30–35 % of the frame height) behind the person, clipped by the edge.
- Flat shapes — including the containers that crop photos — are derived from the geometry of the
  user's logo (corners, curves, letters, line thickness). A sharp-cornered logo produces
  angular containers, not automatically blobs, pills, or rounded frames.
- The wordmark and stage letters always belong to the user's brand.

**Color**
- The user's brand color appears as full fields, alternating with light/dark neutrals that also
  come from the brand. 1–2 accents are taken from the brand palette; if the brand has only one
  color, choose the accent with a reason written in the style brief — do not copy the example's
  color pair. Accents only on stickers, labels, big words, and rays.
- Flat shapes with no gradients; real photos saturated; soft 3D illustrations may carry subtle
  shading.
- The background changes often (in the example ±12 times per 30 s) and ALWAYS with a trigger:
  an object covers the lens, a push into a color plane, a UI action, an icon growing, a field
  sweeping, a cut on the beat. At most one dark scene.
- Flat fields are legitimate here without texture because they change quickly and are always
  filled with moving objects (an exception to `techniques.md` §7d).

**Rhythm**
- 30 s, ±25 shots, an average of ±1.2 s. People introduction acts 1.5–3 s; photo montages
  (locations, merchandise, stage letters) 0.3–0.8 s on the beat.
- Stickers, pills, and small shapes pop elastically (back.out); color fields snap fast
  (0.15–0.3 s); camera push/zoom only to enter a color plane.

**Cautions**
- Photos of people come from the user, are generated via MCP, or are licensed stock — ask the
  user first; the full flow is in the "Photos in openers" section. Clean cut-outs with no
  background residue.
- Do not copy the reference's shapes, letters, props, or sentences — the stage letters,
  stickers, and giant words are derived from the user's brand and audience.

## Photos in openers — asset sources, preparation, animation

An opener does not have to be icons or illustrations: photos of real people, places, and products
make a brand feel close (concept 17; also fitting for 3, 4, 6, 9, 13). The skill must not
fabricate photos that are legitimate, so the source is decided TOGETHER with the user before building.

**1. Ask for the photo source first** (one question, before the final style brief)
- **Photos from the user** (priority): photos of the brand, ambassadors, users, the business
  premises, or the product. Make sure the user has the right to use them and the people in the
  photos agree to appear.
- **Generate via the Higgsfield MCP** if the user has no photos and approves.
- **Licensed stock** supplied or approved by the user.
- With no photos: choose another concept or the illustration render style (`techniques.md`
  §7e) — do not quietly swap photos for illustrations that were not agreed on.

**2. Shot list** — write it in the style brief before requesting or generating photos: what photo
each act needs (a person popping from below, cheering, pointing sideways, holding a phone,
hands holding the product, a usage location, merchandise), the orientation, and the empty space
for text. Users who supply their own photos are helped by this list too.

**3. Generate via MCP (if approved)**
- `generate_image` with one style block for all shots: studio photo, the same lighting, a plain
  background that contrasts with clothing and hair, framing (full body / half body), pose,
  expression. A plain background makes cut-outs easy.
- Consistency of people: one persona per role; use the same reference/character image for all
  poses so faces and clothing do not change between acts.
- Fictional faces — do not imitate real people, celebrities, or brand employees without
  permission. Clothing may be in brand colors; the brand's logo and text are added in code
  (stickers/layers), not generated, because text produced by generators is often broken.
- Generate one batch per style, then show the results to the user before animating.
- `remove_background` → transparent PNG. If the subject is erased too, or the hair edges are
  ruined, regenerate with a more contrasting background — do not force it.
- Location/usage-place photos: eye-level documentary photos, without text and brand names;
  brand markers are attached in code.

**4. Photo preparation** (from the user or generated)
- Resolution ≥ 1.5–2× the largest display size — zooms and camera pushes need spare pixels.
- Trim transparent margins (alpha bbox) so the CSS size = the content; check for a white halo
  on the cut edges at 200 % magnification.
- Match the color feel between photos with one thin shared grade (e.g. `filter:
  saturate() contrast()` or a low-opacity `soft-light` palette tint) so photos from different
  sources feel like one campaign.
- A soft contact shadow for people who "stand" on the background; no shadow for people clipped
  by the bottom edge.

**5. Animating photos so they do not sit still**
- A person pops from below with overshoot (`back.out`) and a small rotation −4° → 0°, then a
  gentle drift (scale 1 → 1.02) while held.
- Parallax in 2–3 layers: flat shapes behind, the person in the middle, stickers/pills in front;
  the camera shifts and each layer moves at a different speed.
- Containers cropping photos: the photo is masked by a blooming blob/rounded frame. A person
  may leave the frame (head/hands past the edge) using two copies: one masked by the container,
  one a top-part cut-out with no mask.
- Going through brand letters/shapes: the whole shape behind the person and a copy of the shape
  in front, partly masked, so the person seems to pose through the shape.
- Montage on the beat: the person's position and scale stay fixed, the photo changes every
  0.3–0.8 s.
- Ken Burns alone is still rejected (SKILL.md structural prohibition); full-background photos use
  parallax or a camera that genuinely moves.

**6. Video as a supplement** — the source is asked about the same way (the user's file, generated
via MCP with a cost check, or stock). Examples in an opener: a product screen recording inside a
device mockup in a SaaS promo, B-roll of people using the product inside a shape container, one
emphasis clip between animated scenes. The clip is placed as a footage layer bound to the
timeline (`techniques.md` §9b), not a video that plays on its own.

## Product tour interleaved with claims — layout, color, rhythm

Concept 19 suits apps/SaaS whose strength lies in daily-use flows (dashboards, directories,
profiles, reports). The real UI is used like a tour, and every few seconds the tour pauses on one
short claim on a brand color field. Pick the parts that fit.

**An example order** (not a script — skeleton law #7)
1. A title with an object in the gap between words: two title words flank a small stack of UI
   cards, a small sentence beneath. The camera snap-zooms to the cards (strong radial blur,
   ±0.5 s) then returns to the same composition.
2. The title dissolves into a full dashboard seen up close; the camera pulls back slowly then
   pushes into one widget.
3. A card is lifted out: the UI dims to gray, the widget rises and floats, and the background
   behind it turns into a brand-color gradient; a second card flies in with 3D rotation and lands
   beneath it; its contents fill in staggered.
4. The cards fade upward; a two-beat claim appears word by word on the color field — two very
   short sentences, the second line following.
5. The claim dims → the UI returns: a cursor opens a menu, a list appears, one row is chosen.
6. A detail panel appears over the list; the list dims and the background returns to a color field.
7. A split screen: left, a light panel holding a card fragment; right, a color field holding the
   second claim word by word; then the split closes into a close zoom on a button clicked by the cursor.
8. A full detail page; the camera slides to one section.
9. The UI explodes into a diagram: that section's columns detach into cards connected by lines
   (parent → child) on a pale background; one card grows, the others blur.
10. A third claim above two analytics cards whose bars and numbers count up.
11. Dark to a deep color field; the wordmark shrinks from blurred to sharp, a tagline word by
    word, faint concentric rings in the background.

**Layout**
- Title/claim on one central axis, neutral semibold grotesk, 1–2 short lines (2–4 words per
  line), line height ±6–8 % of the frame height; the intro sentence ±3 %.
- The UI appears at two scales: full screen (the tour) and detached cards at 25–40 % of the frame
  width (highlights), rounded corners, soft shadow.
- Split screen 50/50 or 55/45; text is always on the color half, the UI on the light half.
- Card diagram: the parent top-center, 3 children aligned below, thin connecting lines.

**Color**
- Three background states that change per act, always triggered by the UI (dimming, a lifted
  card, a split): warm white/cream for titles and the tour (±40 % of the duration), a brand-color
  gradient dark → light at the bottom for claims and detached cards (±45 %), a very pale version
  of the brand color for diagrams and analytics (±15 %).
- The brand color is also the color of buttons/actions in the UI, so the color fields feel born
  from the product.
- Other accents only from UI data (bars, small labels).
- Closing: the deepest brand color field, flat with faint rings.

**Rhythm**
- 25–30 s, 10–11 acts of 2–5 s; only 1–2 cuts, the rest carried by the UI.
- One energy hit at the opening (the snap zoom); the rest calm, ease in-out 0.8–1.2 s.
- Claims rise word by word from blurred (±0.12–0.15 s per word), hold 1.5–2 s, exit by dimming
  ±50 % first then disappearing.
- Interleaved: UI → claim → UI → claim → UI → claim → logo; never two claims in a row.

## UI animation for apps & SaaS — layout, color, rhythm

For an app or SaaS product, the most convincing proof is that the product WORKS. Whatever the
parent concept (5, 9, 10, 15, 16…), an app/SaaS opener has at least one live UI act:
the UI is assembled, used, and produces something — not a still screenshot that is
zoomed. Concept 16 makes UI animation the backbone of the whole video.

**Only that much is mandatory.** Everything else in this section — cursors, avatars, badges,
carousels, devices, layout numbers and color proportions — is a repertoire, not a recipe. Pick
what suits the product; the palette, background, and style are still derived from the brand
(Law #3).

**Building the UI for animation**
- Rebuild the user's product UI as simplified DOM/vector components, not a screenshot: the titles,
  buttons, and labels that carry the story stay legible; other small text becomes rounded gray lines.
- Components, colors, terms, and fonts are taken from the user's own product (UI font = product
  font). If the user has no UI yet, design a minimal plausible UI for the category and show it
  first together with the style brief.
- One screen = one task: one panel, one action, one result. Unused parts are cropped out of the
  frame or blurred.
- Panels float with no device frame: a white card, rounded corners, a wide soft shadow that falls
  far below (not an edge line). Devices (laptop/phone) are kept for the statement and closing acts.
- The action trigger is a free choice: a regular cursor, a finger/tap for mobile apps, or no
  cursor at all (the UI reacts by itself). One OPTIONAL variation: a persona cursor — a cursor
  arrow with the user's round avatar beside it, used when it matters to show WHO is working
  (the avatar from the user or newly made).
- Data is always alive: bars fill in sequence, tiered progress, running numbers, list rows
  appearing staggered (deterministic, `techniques.md` §9).
- The camera follows the interaction when needed: before an important click the camera moves in on
  the target area, the click reads large, then pulls back/slides to the result (`techniques.md`
  §8). Not on every click — a calm UI act may stay wide, as long as the video does not feel
  static.

**An example order — concept 16** (not a script — skeleton law #7; the flow is taken from the user's product)
1. Old tools scattered about: windows and files floating on a tilted 3D plane, the camera drifting.
2. The product icon is born from behind the pile: the symbol spins alone, then an app-icon
   container grows behind it.
3. The icon rises and shrinks; a title appears; the UI is assembled (an input, suggestion chips);
   an action trigger (cursor, finger, or the UI itself) runs it.
4. The panel blooms into the next step: upload/choose → process with progress.
5. The result: a document/dashboard with data filling in; the camera slides slowly.
6. The result locks into a badge that shrinks until only an icon remains; integration badges
   attach.
7. Who uses it: role avatars + label pills popping, scattered.
8. The ecosystem: windows and file icons pop, then curve into a carousel around the icon.
9. The content swaps, the container stays: one document, the use-case title changing 3–5 times.
10. A benefit sentence + a running row of capability chips.
11. A side of a giant 3D icon passes as a color field → the icon retreats → an integration burst
    & orbit.
12. A positioning statement beside the icon; a device enters pushing the sentence out, the icon
    flies onto its screen, UI windows come out of the screen.
13. A problem act (optional): one big dark symbolic object + a problem sentence.
14. Closing: a device on a slowly turning base showing the real UI, the icon + an invitation
    sentence word by word beside it; the icon grows to the lens as a bumper.

**UI motion menu** (pick what the product flow demands; not all of them)
- **Component assembly**: title → the input bar blooms from the center → the placeholder is
  typed → a row of suggestion chips follows staggered from below.
- **A click you can feel**: the cursor arrives with ease-out, stops ±0.2 s, the button is
  pressed (scale .92 → 1), only then the screen reacts; if the click matters, the camera moves
  in before the click and pulls back/slides afterward.
- **Panel blooms into the next step**: the clicked panel grows and its contents become the next
  step's screen — one container, not a slide change.
- **File flies to the dropzone**: a file thumbnail is carried by the cursor to the upload area;
  once dropped, analysis progress rows fill one by one.
- **Charts fill in sequence**: horizontal bars grow from the left with ±0.08 s stagger; the data
  color grades from the brand color to a second color.
- **Windows floating on a 3D tilt**: UI windows and table cards on a tilted plane
  (rotateX 20–35°, rotateZ −10…−20°); file-type/integration icons attached to their corners.
- **Window carousel**: windows curve into a cylinder that rotates around the product icon; the
  icon flips in the center.
- **Content swaps, container stays**: one panel stays still; the title above it changes with a
  vertical roll (the old one rises fading, the new one rises from below) and its icon glyph flips —
  every ±1.5 s. Many use cases without lining up cards.
- **Running chips**: 2–3 rows of capability/template chips shifting horizontally and slowly,
  alternating direction per row; each chip has a color accent line on its left edge.
- **Role pop + label pills**: round avatars pop one at a time at asymmetric scattered positions;
  from each avatar a thin line extends and becomes a role label pill.
- **Integration badges**: the product and connected services each in a round white badge; the
  second badge blooms from a point then attaches beside the first.
- **Integration burst & orbit**: service icons burst out of the product icon, float scattered,
  then are sucked back in.
- **Thick 3D app icon**: the product icon has a side thickness visible as it rotates; used as a
  chapter divider and a closing bumper.
- **Device carrying the UI**: a device enters from the edge pushing the sentence out; the icon
  flips and flies to its screen; UI windows come out of the screen and float around it.
- **Card lifted out**: the UI dims to gray, one widget rises and floats in front, the background
  behind it turns into a brand color field.
- **UI explodes into a diagram**: one section's columns detach into cards connected by
  parent → child lines.
- **Depth focus**: one card grows sharp, the other cards shrink and blur.
- **Split screen UI / claim**: a light half holding a UI fragment, a color half holding the claim;
  the split closes into a zoom on the next detail.

**Layout** (starting-point numbers, not targets)
- Example: a single very light gray background plane in almost every act and pure white UI cards
  lifted only by shadow. A dark, brand-colored, or gradient background is equally legitimate if
  the brand asks for it; the background still moves (§7c) and may change color per chapter.
- One focus object per moment: the product icon ±15–27 % of the frame height, the demo UI panel
  60–90 % of the frame width, a device ±45–55 % of the width.
- Text in one style: a regular-weight geometric grotesk, sentence case, almost black; line
  height ±6 % of the frame height, 1–2 lines. Text is not stacked on top of the UI — it stands at
  the SIDE of an object (left of the icon/device, or above a panel as a title); the product
  icon may be the "last word" of the sentence.
- Chips and buttons that are part of the UI may be many; that is the interface, not decorative
  badges.

**Color** (starting-point proportions; the colors come from the brand)
- Example: ±86 % of the screen area white/very light gray, ±7 % medium gray (UI text lines,
  shadows), ±2 % dark (text), ±4 % brand color (icons, buttons, pills, bars). Other colors only
  from data (chart gradients) and integration icons.
- The brand color fills the screen only once or twice, as the surface of a giant 3D icon passing
  in front of the camera — that is the chapter marker.
- One large dark object may appear once in the problem act so the emotional contrast is felt.

**Rhythm**
- 50–60 s, 12–16 acts of 2–6 s; continuous music with no VO — everything reads without sound.
- Almost every transition is carried by the UI (transition menu below); hard cuts at most two,
  each marking a chapter.
- Sentences appear word by word from light gray/blurred to sharp dark (±0.12–0.2 s per word);
  when a sentence changes, the new word enters while the old one fades and the line shifts along.
- No highlight words: emphasis comes from the icon beside the sentence, word order, and pauses.

**Cautions**
- Third-party service logos only if the user's product really integrates with them and the user
  agrees to show them; otherwise use generic file-type icons (document, table, letter, chart).
- Terms, sentences, template names, and data are taken from the user's product — do not copy the
  reference UI's contents.
- A real screenshot may be used as the content of a device screen in the closing act; the demo in
  the core acts stays animatable components.

## Transition menu carried by objects

Cut-free transitions carried by an object; they complement the `techniques.md` §4c menu.

- **The next scene's object becomes the wipe**: the new scene's device/door/package rises from
  the frame edge covering the old scene, and the camera is already in its new scene.
- **Out of the screen**: the chosen item lifts toward the camera, the screen curves or folds
  away, and the item is now a real object in a new world.
- **Shape match cut + render style change**: a colored object on a dark background → cut to the
  same shape as an outline on a white background → it regains color as the act proceeds.
- **Pull back to reveal the destination**: the subject shrinks to the center while the environment
  assembles around it (a porch, a door, a room) — the object turns out to have already arrived.
- **A collecting hand**: a hand/foot enters the frame and takes the object; that movement closes
  one act and opens the next.
- **Tiered color field + logo flipping color**: a palette color plane rises/sweeps in two steps
  (the secondary briefly, then the main) and BECOMES the background of the new act; the logo
  stays in place, its color flipping exactly at the field's edge. Not a decorative block passing
  through — the field stays.
- **Through the symbol**: the symbol grows quickly to the lens with blur; its light becomes the
  background of the next act.
- **A parade pushes**: a row of objects enters from the side and bumps the text out of the frame.
- **Thin spin → a color card opens**: an object spins until edge-on then vanishes; a color field
  card appears edge-on and spins open to fill the frame.
- **Pages rising stacked**: large pages/cards rise from below in turn covering the act; the last
  blank page becomes the clean background.
- **A growing point**: the new act begins from one color point that grows into a symbol.
- **A container blooms / shrinks**: a UI slot detaches as an empty card, blooms to the center, then
  starts playing the result; at the end the card shrinks (its contents keep being cropped in) →
  a pill → a dot, and the next sentence appears at that dot.
- **The result recedes into a sentence**: the work output shrinks, blurs, fades into the
  background while a sentence appears beneath it — a time overlap of ±0.4 s.
- **Zoom into a UI fragment**: the camera moves in on the UI part the cursor is using and shifts
  along with the cursor; other parts leave the frame by themselves.
- **Dim then swap**: the sentence turns light gray first, then disappears; the new sentence
  appears in the same position.
- **Erase-type**: the old text is erased letter by letter from the left, the new text (e.g. an
  address → the product name) typed in the same position.
- **A shrinking badge**: the UI screen locks inside a round/serrated badge that shrinks; its
  contents keep being cropped until only the product icon remains in the center of the badge.
- **Panel blooms into the next step**: a button is clicked → the same panel grows to fill the
  frame and its contents become the next screen.
- **Windows curve into a carousel**: flat windows curve into a rotating cylinder; when it
  stops, one window straightens into the next act's panel.
- **A 3D icon's side as a wipe**: a brand color plane sweeps the frame and turns out to be the
  surface of a giant 3D icon that spins then recedes; the new act is already behind it.
- **A device pushes the sentence**: a device enters from the edge, the sentence is pushed out
  fading, the icon flies to the device's screen.
- **Icon flip swaps content**: the icon spins until edge-on; on its far side the glyph and title
  have already changed.
- **An object exits to the corner**: the old act's big object flies to the top corner and its
  sentence blurs out, while the new act's object has already risen from below.
- **A pill blooms into a photo container**: the label pill in the center grows into a blob/frame
  holding the next person.
- **Push into a color plane**: the camera pushes into a colored frame until its color becomes
  the next act's background.
- **A UI action changes the background**: a toggle/button is activated and the background color
  changes following its state.
- **UI enters the phone**: a big UI element shrinks into the screen of a phone rising in a hand.
- **Object to the lens**: an object flies to the camera with motion blur and opens a color field.
- **A container bursts its contents**: a folder/box drops and lands, then its contents burst out.
- **A photo card fills the frame**: one card in a collage grows into the next scene.
- **Icon becomes a field**: an app icon grows past the frame edge; its color becomes the background.
- **Alternating color fields**: 3–4 palette fields snap/sweep 0.2–0.3 s with the logo staying in
  the center, its color flipping following the field.
- **A fixed marker, the world changes**: a sticker/logo in the same position while the photo
  behind it changes on each beat.
- **Through a shape's hole**: the camera pushes into a hole (a keyhole, a portal, a letter) until
  the dark inside becomes the next act's background.
- **Logo shape echo**: the logo silhouette repeated in concentric layers with alternating colors,
  then the layers collapse inward into a single logo.
- **A guiding orb**: a small orb/light bounces to the next location; the camera follows it, the
  place where the orb lands is the new scene.
- **Flat becomes 3D**: a flat circle/shape spins and turns out to be a volumetric object (a coin,
  a button) — a render style change mid-motion.
- **A fixed point, the background changes**: a composition of flat points/shapes stays in place
  while the background color changes beneath it.
- **Logo shape mask opens**: the logo shape (a letter, a circle, a cross) becomes a window that
  grows to reveal the next scene.
- **Tiles flipping**: the frame splits into a grid of tiles that flip one by one into the new scene.
- **Vertical panels shifting**: 3–4 side-by-side illustration panels shift horizontally; one panel
  widens into a full scene.
- **A brush writes the logo**: a gradient stroke draws a line that turns out to be the logo's
  lettering; stickers attach afterward.
- **Objects fall onto a podium**: objects drop onto blocks rising from below; the blocks merge into
  one base.
- **Snap zoom then return**: the camera snaps to an object with strong radial blur then returns to
  the original composition, slightly changed — only once, usually in the opening.
- **UI dims → a card lifted**: not a cut; the next act's background appears behind the card lifted
  out of the UI.
- **A split closes into a zoom**: the split screen narrows to one side while the camera moves in on
  the next button.
- **Claim dims → UI dissolves in**: the sentence drops to ±50 % opacity, then a bright UI dissolves
  in from the center.

## Menu for presenting features (an automatic replacement for "three tiles")

- **One feature demoed**, the rest glimpsed for 0.4 s at the end.
- **A word swaps** in the same sentence: "Edit ___" → photo / video / voice.
- **An object transforms**: feature A's icon/object morphs into feature B.
- **Cards shuffled or thrown** one by one from a hand/pile.
- **Orbit**: feature icons circle the product then enter it.
- **A mockup in use**: scrolled/clicked, the feature lights up in place.
- **A diegetic checklist** ticked by actions in the world.
- **A before/after dividing line** that shifts per feature.
- **A screen that wakes then is used**: a UI skeleton appears, product cards fill the grid one by
  one, a cursor chooses — the chosen item then leaves the screen as an object.
- **Collected by a hand**: feature/product cards float in from the frame edge, a hand (or a paw)
  collects them one by one so the pile grows.
- **Personality stickers**: chat bubbles/labels in the language of the brand's audience community
  burst at an object's corner then are pulled back — brand personality without sentences.
- **Fast content swaps**: one container (icon/card) swaps its contents several times in
  < 1 s then stops on the brand's own.
- **Brand guideline pages**: the logo, palette, typography, graphics, and applications appear as
  big pages rising stacked.
- **A claim → how → result cycle per feature**: each feature gets one sentence, one moment of the
  UI in use, one blooming result — repeated, not lined up.
- **The cursor as actor**: an asset dragged to a slot, a menu opened, an option ticked; the
  product's real UI with no device frame.
- **UI flow used by a persona**: assemble → click → panel blooms → process → result (the UI
  motion menu is in the UI animation section).
- **Content swaps, container stays**: one still panel, the use-case title + icon glyph changing
  every ±1.5 s.
- **Running chips**: rows of capability chips shifting horizontally, alternating direction.
- **Role pop + label pills**: who uses the product, as scattered labeled avatars.
- **Integration burst**: connected services burst from the product icon then are sucked back in.
- **A giant word swapping along with the person**: the user's role/need as a big word behind the
  photo; the word, the person, and the screen contents swap together.
- **A container bursts out features**: a folder/box lands, feature illustrations burst out.
- **The brand in the real world**: the brand marker stays fixed, usage locations change quickly.
- **A 3D object flanked by small text**: one big object in the center, small sentence fragments to
  its left and right.
- **A table being assembled**: objects from the product category drop and arrange into one
  table/collage.
- **A parade of lifestyle objects**: a row of 3D objects from the user's category shifting
  horizontally on a dark background, color dots floating between them.
- **A word pattern as background**: the brand word repeated as a shifting outline pattern; one
  word filled in full as an object passes.
- **Tour interleaved with claims**: each feature = one UI tour fragment + one two-beat claim on a
  brand color field.
- **An icon tile grid** (the `techniques.md` §8 recipe) — the count follows the features, not
  always three.

Two openers in a row do not use the same method.

## Opening menu (the first 3 seconds)

- **In media res**: the object is already moving in the first frame.
- **Sound first**: a beat/effect, the visual following one beat later.
- **A giant word clipped by the edge**, the camera pulling back to reveal the whole sentence.
- **A line drawing** that turns out to be the product/logo shape.
- **A close-up texture** that turns out to be part of the product.
- **A question typed diegetically** on a world object (not a floating input box).
- **Dark → one light → a world**.
- **The problem immediately**: the frame opens in the middle of the chaos.
- **A gray world, one colored subject**: a monochrome line environment, only the actor in the
  brand color is colored; the brand name is written large as part of the room.
- **An unexpected actor**: the first frame immediately shows who the real user is (a hand/paw on
  a device) — the humor reads in one second.
- **An empty frame, a motif object rising from the edge**: the logo shape made thick and layered,
  its layers following one by one — also prepared for a loop closing.
- **The result first**: the first frame is the product's output, very close and blurred, which
  recedes to make room for the claim sentence.
- **Old tools scattered**: windows and files floating tilted in 3D on a light background; the
  product icon is born from behind the pile.
- **The symbol first, the container follows**: the logo symbol spins alone, then an app-icon
  container grows behind it.
- ✦ **A stretching word**: one short word stretches (letters repeated or elongated) until it
  fills the frame width. A distinctive moment — use it only when that word really carries a
  sense of length/continuity for the product, with a new word and a new way of stretching.
- **A point of light in the dark**: one point blooms into rotating gradient shape layers.
- **Kinetic title assembling**: title letters enter separately from different directions then lock
  into the title, with small 3D objects floating around it.
- **An object in the word gap**: a two-word title flanks a small stack of cards/product; a snap
  zoom to the object then return.

There are eight text entrances — rising, descending, left, right, from depth, mask reveal,
word by word, typed. Choose from the meaning of the sentence; two scenes in a row do not use
the same entrance.

## Closing menu

- **The logo is born from the last object** (a morph or a closing shape).
- **The camera pulls back**: the world turns out to be the logo shape.
- **The key word of the last sentence is replaced by the logo**.
- **A diegetic CTA**: a button in the UI/world object is pressed, not a floating CTA text.
- **A seamless loop**: the last frame connects to the first frame.
- **A firm stillness**: all motion stops at once, one sentence, cut.
- **Receiving**: the hero object has arrived, the recipient's hand/foot enters the frame; the brand
  is enough on the object's label, no end card.
- **Callback to the opening**: the logo takes the form/layers of the opening's motif object then
  exits through the edge where that object came in; the last frame is empty, the same as the
  first frame.
- **Address → name**: a site address typed, erased from the left, the product name typed in the
  same place, still for 2–3 s, fading into the background.
- **A device on a base + the icon to the lens**: a device with the real UI turning slowly on a
  base, an invitation sentence word by word beside it; the icon grows to the lens into a color
  field, then recedes as a bumper.
- ✦ **Brand letters as a stage**: people posing through giant brand letters/symbol, colors and
  people changing per beat. A distinctive moment — only when the user's brand shape is truly
  strong as a stage, and not combined with its example's closing.
- **Through a portal to the logo**: the camera enters a shape's hole; in the dark beyond, the logo
  appears.
- **An object on a podium**: the main object drops and lands on a podium assembled from blocks.
- **A wordmark shrinking from blur**: a deep brand color field, a big blurred wordmark shrinking to
  sharp, a tagline word by word, faint concentric rings.

Logo + CTA stacked in the center only when the brief asks for an explicit CTA.
